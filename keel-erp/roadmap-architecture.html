Product plan · v0.1

Keel

An integrated operating core for small distributors and light manufacturers — the part of SAP that actually earns its keep (one ledger every module posts to, live) without the consultants, the modules you'll never touch, or the eighteen-month rollout.

The core idea, running. A working proof of concept where a sales order posts straight to the general ledger — no batch job, no month-end close.

01Roadmap

Seven phases, each ending in a real exit criterion rather than a ship date. Target customer for v1: 20–150 employees, $2M–$25M revenue, inventory-heavy — distributors and light manufacturers currently stuck between spreadsheets-plus-QuickBooks and an SAP Business One implementation they can't justify.

Foundation & validation

Weeks 1–6
Prove the wedge before writing the ledger engine.
  • 15–20 discovery interviews with distributors/light manufacturers on QuickBooks + spreadsheets, or an SAP B1 install they've outgrown or resent
  • Two chart-of-accounts templates built from real client trial balances (distribution, light manufacturing)
  • Five willingness-to-pay conversations to pressure-test the pricing hypothesis below
Exit criteria10 signed LOIs or paid pilot commitments

Core ledger MVP

Months 2–4
The integrated financial core, live — this is the whole bet.
  • Double-entry GL engine: event-sourced, append-only journal
  • AP/AR, bank feed reconciliation (Plaid)
  • Real-time financial statements — no batch close
  • Multi-entity, single currency
Exit criteria3 design-partner customers running live books on Keel

Procurement & inventory

Months 4–6
Close the loop SAP MM used to own.
  • PO → receipt → 3-way match → AP invoice
  • Reorder points, moving-average costing
  • Vendor management, single-level approval workflow
Exit criteriaDesign partners fully off QuickBooks for inventory

Quote-to-cash

Months 6–8
Sales flows straight into the ledger, the same way purchasing does.
  • Quote → sales order → fulfillment → invoice
  • Pricing rules, tax calculation (Avalara or TaxJar)
  • Customer portal for invoice payment
Exit criteria10 paying customers, $15–20k MRR

Productized onboarding

Months 8–10
Kill the implementation consultant; keep the thing they were valuable for.
  • Guided self-serve onboarding wizard built on the CoA templates from Phase 0
  • Flat-fee white-glove tier: $2,500 fixed, 2-week SLA, no hourly billing
  • Vertical-specific report packs (distribution vs. manufacturing)
Exit criteriaTime-to-live-books under 10 business days, no implementation consultant involved

Light manufacturing

Months 10–13
The moat module — the depth QuickBooks-tier tools can't follow into.
  • BOMs, work orders, routing
  • Job costing tied to the GL in real time, not at period-end
  • WIP tracking
Exit criteria5 manufacturing customers running job costing live

Embedded finance & marketplace

Month 13+
Margin expansion beyond per-seat revenue.
  • Embedded payments (take rate), payroll partner integration, invoice-financing referral
  • Public API, automation layer, third-party app marketplace with a take rate
  • Second vertical evaluation — professional services or field service
Exit criteriaNon-seat revenue exceeds 20% of total revenue

Downmarket: Keel Lite

Month 16+
A cheap, self-serve front door for businesses too small for full Keel today — with a growth path into it.
  • Same ledger engine, radically simplified surface: GL, AP/AR, bank feed, basic invoicing, simple stock counts — no job costing, no multi-entity, no 3-way match
  • One-click industry presets (retail, salon/service, single-location restaurant, solo contractor) — a fully populated chart of accounts and dashboard in under 15 minutes, no sales call
  • Flat pricing ($29–49/mo, not per-seat), mobile-first, subsidized in part by embedded-payments volume from Phase 06
  • In-app graduation signals — SKU count, headcount, order volume — that prompt an upgrade to full Keel with zero data migration, since it's the same schema underneath
Exit criteria1,000+ self-serve accounts and a measurable graduation rate into full Keel within 12 months

02Architecture

Five decisions carry the whole system. Everything else — which job queue, which ORM — is replaceable; these aren't.

Ledger as source of truth

Every business event, in every module, becomes an immutable double-entry journal entry the moment it happens. There is no nightly batch and no "close the books" ritual standing between an action and its true financial effect.

Event-sourced core, CRUD periphery

The ledger itself is append-only. Operational modules — sales, procurement, inventory — can use ordinary CRUD, but any state change that has financial consequence must emit a ledger event synchronously.

Multi-tenant from day one

Postgres row-level security keyed on tenant_id, not schema-per-tenant. Simpler operations at the scale Keel actually runs at; schema-per-tenant is a cost worth avoiding until there's a specific reason not to.

API-first

Every UI action is a call to a publicly-shaped API. This is what lets Phase 6's automation layer and marketplace exist later without a rewrite of the core.

Boring, provable technology

Postgres, not a bespoke ledger database. A well-understood job queue, not a custom event bus. Microservices get introduced when a real scaling problem shows up, not before.

One core, two front doors

Keel Lite (Phase 07) is not a fork — it's the same multi-tenant ledger and schema behind a constrained UI and a more aggressive onboarding wizard. Upgrading from Lite to full Keel unlocks modules; it never migrates data.

03Core data model

EntityPurposeKey fields
accountChart of accounts linecode, name, type, normal_balance
journal_entryImmutable, append-only postingid, tenant_id, date, memo, source_type, source_id
journal_lineOne debit or credit leg of an entryentry_id, account_code, debit, credit
itemInventory SKUsku, name, qty_on_hand, avg_cost, price
sales_orderQuote-to-cash record; posts on fulfillmentcustomer, sku, qty, price, terms, status
purchase_orderProcure-to-pay record; posts on receiptvendor, sku, qty, cost, status
tenantRow-level security boundaryid, plan, created_at

The pattern that repeats: sales_order and purchase_order never touch account balances directly. Fulfilling or receiving one calls the ledger engine, which is the only thing allowed to write a journal_entry. The POC linked below implements exactly this shape in miniature.

Security note RLS per tenant, encryption at rest, and an audit trail that comes for free: every journal entry already carries an actor and a timestamp, because that's what a ledger is.
